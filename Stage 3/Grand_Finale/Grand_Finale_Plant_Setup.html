Driver wiring notes

  • Wire by the printed names. With the driver's header facing the PCB, its pins read DIR1 · PWM1 · DIR2 · PWM2 · GND from left to right, the same order as PCB pins 1 to 5, so a straight 5-wire cable works. Seen from the other side the order is reversed (GND · PWM2 · DIR2 · PWM1 · DIR1), so check the labels before you plug in.
  • On MOTOR_DRIVER2 the PCB prints DIR3 PWM3 DIR4 PWM4. They go to DIR1 PWM1 DIR2 PWM2 on DRV2, because each driver numbers its own two channels 1 and 2.
  • PCB pin 6 is a second GND. The driver has only one GND pin, so pin 6 has no wire.
  • Connect the DIR wires even though the PCB holds them LOW. A floating DIR input could flip a pump's direction at random.
  • The green terminal is the driver's 12 V input. Check + and − with a meter at PCB P1 and P2 before you connect it, because a reversed supply can destroy the driver. P3 to P5 stay spare.
  • Each channel drives one 3.5 A pump, well under the 10 A rating. The 3.3 V PWM from the ESP32 drives the MDD10A input directly, at the firmware's 20 kHz.
  • If a pump hums but moves no water, swap its two leads at MxA and MxB.

Two hand valves decide where the liquid goes. The firmware cannot see BV-M and BV-J. For the batch: BV-M open, BV-J shut. For flush, priming and calibration: BV-M shut, BV-J open. Every serial message repeats the setting it needs.

8 holes = the 8 hose connections in the kit. Use the 4 nuts on the 4 bottom holes, where the water presses on the joint. The top holes carry no pressure, so a female connector or a snug fit is enough. One washer goes inside and one outside each wall (16 of the 37 washers). Tighten until the washers just compress, and don't crack the plastic.

Control sequence

  1. Set BV-M open and BV-J shut. Stir the base tanks. Press START once.
  2. Yellow: SV2 opens, then P2 runs at full duty. At 270 mL it drops to slow duty, and it stops early at 300 mL minus the learned overrun. SV2 closes.
  3. Green: the same with SV3, P3 and FT3 for 900 mL. Green pushes Yellow's leftover out of the header.
  4. Red: the same with SV1, P1 and FT1 for 1,800 mL.
  5. Water chase: SV4 opens and P4 runs for the set time, pushing Red's leftover from the header into T-5.
  6. The report prints the target, metered volume and error for each colour. The controller then waits.
  7. Flush: shut BV-M, open BV-J, press START again. P4 sends flush water through SV4 and the whole header into the 2 L jug only.
  8. Before the next batch, set BV-J shut and BV-M open. Empty the jug when it nears 2 L.

Assumptions in this drawing

  • Only 4 solenoid valves are on the table, so the mixing tank and jug outlets use hand ball valves. The batch itself still runs from one START press.
  • The colour tanks have no ball valve: the normally closed SV at the end of each line isolates them. The fourth ball valve is spare.
  • Supply tanks sit on a shelf above the pumps, so the pumps start flooded and self-prime.
  • Flow sensors have hose barbs (as in the photo), wire colours red = V, black or brown = G, third = S. Confirm during priming.
  • The fitting counts are matched to the kit list. Check the parts in hand before cutting tube.
